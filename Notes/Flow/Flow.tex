Recall the following result about change of variable: Suppose $z \sim \pi(z)$ and $f(x) = z$ for some invertible $f$. The distribution of $x \sim p(x)$ can be determined by 
\ali{p(x) &= \pi(z) \left|\frac{dz}{dx}\right| \\
          &= \pi(f(x)) | f^{'} (x) |.}
Its multivariate version is 
\ali{p(x) =  \pi(f(x)) \left| \det \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \right) \right|,}
where $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ is the Jocabian matrix.\\
Suppose data $x \sim p(x)$ and latent variable $z \sim \pi(z)$. {\bf Normalizing Flow Model} uses a series of invertible maps, $f_1, ..., f_K$, with
\ali{x = z_0 \xrightarrow{f_1} z_1 \xrightarrow{f_2} z_2 \xrightarrow{f_3} ... \xrightarrow{f_K} z_K = z.}  
Suppose $z_i$ has distribution $p_i$. We have
\ali{p_i(z_i) = p_{i+1}(f_{i+1}(z_i)) \left| \det \left( \frac{\partial f_{i+1}}{\partial z_i} \right) \right|.}
Hence, if we put $F = f_K \circ ... \circ f_1$ mapping $x$ to $z$,
\ali{p(x) = p_0(z_0) &= p_K(F(z_0)) \prod_{i=0}^{K-1}  \left| \det \left( \frac{\partial f_{i+1}}{\partial z_i} \right) \right| \\
                    &= \pi(F(x)) \prod_{i=0}^{K-1}  \left| \det \left( \frac{\partial f_{i+1}}{\partial z_i} \right) \right|.}
The log-likelihood therefore is
\ali{\log p(x) = \log \pi(F(x)) + \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} \log \left| \det \left( \frac{\partial f_{i+1}}{\partial z_i} \right) \right|.}
Necessary conditions of the design of the invertible maps $f_1, ..., f_K$ include the following:\\
(1) they are easy to compute;\\
(2) the determinants of the Jocobians are easy to compute;\\
(3) their inverses are easy to compute.\\

{\bf Real NVP (Real-valued Non-Volume Preserving)} uses invertible maps satisfying the requirements above. Suppose data $x$ and latent variable $z$ are in $\R^D$. Let $1 \leq d < D$ and $s, t: \R^d \ra \R^{D-d}$ be two neural networks. Define the map $f$ from $x$ to $z$ by
\ali{f: x &\ra z \\
    z_{1:d} &= x_{1:d} \\
    z_{d+1:D} &= x_{d+1:D} \odot \exp(s(x_{1:d})) + t(x_{1:d}),}
where $\odot$ is element-wise product.\\
The inverse is easy to compute:
\ali{x_{1:d} &= z_{1:d} \\
    x_{d+1:D} &= (z_{d+1:D} - t(z_{1:d})) \odot \exp(-s(z_{1:d})).}
The Jocobian matrix is 
\ali{\begin{bmatrix} I_d & O_{d \times (D-d)} \\ * & \text{diag}(\exp(s(x_{1:d}))) \end{bmatrix},}
and its determinant is 
\ali{\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{D-d} s(x_{1:d})_j\right).}
We also note that the partitioning can be programmed using a binary mask $b \in \set{0, 1}^D$: 
\ali{ z = b \odot x + (1 - b) \odot \left(x \odot \exp(s(b \odot x)) + t(b \odot x)\right).}
The inverse is 
\ali{x = b \odot z + (1- b) \odot (z - t(b \odot z)) \odot \exp(-s(b \odot z)).}
