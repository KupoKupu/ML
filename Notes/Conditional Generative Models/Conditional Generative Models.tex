\maketitle
Generative models, for example GAN or VAE, can learn conditional distributions.\\
Recall that GAN plays the min-max game:
\ali{\arg \min_\theta \max_\phi \BE_{x, z} \left[ \ln(1 - D_\phi(G_\theta(z))) + \ln D_\phi(x) \right].}
In the context of the MNIST dataset, there are labels $Y$ associated with images $X$ and there are two natural ways to form conditional probabilities between $X$ and $Y$: $p(X | Y = y)$ or $p(Y | X = x)$. Note that $p(Y | X = x)$ is the distribution of the labels given $X = x$, which, in the case of MNIST, is deterministic, and is therefore not very interesting. The other case, $p(X | Y = y)$, is the distribution of $X$ (the images) given a label $y$. If this conditional distribution is learned, we can generate hand-written digits according to a specific label.\\
To learn this conditional distribution, we modify generator $G_\theta(z)$ to include the information of the conditioned label $y$: $G_\theta(y ,z)$. Our discriminator becomes $D_\phi(x, y)$. The min-max game becomes
\ali{\arg \min_\theta \max_\phi \BE_{x, y, z} \left[ \ln(1 - D_\phi(G_\theta(y, z), y)) + \ln D_\phi(x, y) \right].} 
The conditional VAE follows similarly by conditioning on the labels $y$. The prior of the latent variable $z$ remains the same $p_\theta(z)$. We update the likelihood to be $p_\theta(x | z, y)$. The posterior is therefore $q_\phi(z | x, y)$. We reparametrize by $z = g_\phi(x, y, \e)$ for $\e \sim p(\e)$.
